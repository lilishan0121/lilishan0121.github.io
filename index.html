Introduction

Hello! I am a PhD student in Linguistics at McGill University,

My research focuses on how phonological contrasts are maintained, weakened, and reorganized through sound change, with particular interests in individual variation, acoustic cue weighting, and the relationship between speech production and perception.

I am especially interested in how listeners adapt to ongoing sound changes and how linguistic experience shapes the perception of phonetic contrasts. My work draws on experimental phonetics, acoustic analysis, and statistical modeling.

Beyond sound change, I am interested in speech development and the flexibility of phonetic representations across different speaker populations.

Before joining McGill, I received my M.A. in School of Chinese Language and Literature from Beijing Normal University and my B.A. in the department of Chinese Language and Literature from Shandong University.

Research Overview

My research investigates the relationship between phonetic variation, speech perception, and phonological structure, with an emphasis on individual differences and the mechanisms underlying sound change.

Research Interests

Sound Change & Phonological Variation

I investigate how phonetic variation develops into phonological change, focusing on Cantonese tone mergers, individual variability, and the maintenance and reorganization of phonological contrasts.

Speech Perception & Cue Weighting

I am interested in how listeners integrate multiple acoustic cues during speech perception, how cue weights vary across individuals, and how perceptual strategies adapt to different linguistic environments.

Production–Perception Relationships

I examine how phonetic patterns in speech production relate to perceptual categorization, particularly in ongoing sound changes where production and perception may follow different developmental trajectories.

Speech Development & Learning

I am interested in the development and flexibility of phonetic representations, including children's speech production, speech imitation, and the acquisition of phonological contrasts.
